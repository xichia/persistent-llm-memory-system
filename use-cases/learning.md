# Learning

Track concepts, notes, weaknesses, and questions while studying something — so the next session continues the course instead of restarting it.

## Folder shape

Treat a subject as either a `knowledge/<topic>/` folder or a small project:

- **Concepts** — what you understand well enough to reuse (short, in your own words)
- **Notes** — denser capture from a book, course, or paper
- **Weaknesses** — things you still mix up or cannot produce from scratch
- **Questions** — what to ask or drill next
- **Log** — study sessions, if you care about chronology

Weaknesses and questions are the highest-leverage pages. A model that only sees polished notes will quiz you on what you already know.

## Across sessions

Day 1 writes a concept and three questions. Day 4 loads those files and starts from the questions, not from chapter 1. That is the same [long-running-project](long-running-projects.md) pattern, applied to study.

Promote: when a question is truly answered, fold it into the concept note and delete it from the question list. Do not keep every wrong attempt in the structured files.

## What not to inject

- Raw highlights and full transcripts of lectures
- Every flashcard
- Motivational noise

Inject the concept map, the weakness list, and the current questions. Leave the archive on disk.

## Personal overlap

Learning preferences (how you like explanations, what depth, what not to do) live in `person/`, not in every topic folder. The topic folder holds the subject; the person folder holds the learner.
